I joined MRM in July 2019 and walked into an established General Motors design team. OnStar was moving to a new product catalog, and the shopping pages inside Owner Center had to change with it.

I started in a supporting role. By the end of the year, I was leading design on the shopping pages and the cart and checkout process.

## Trust came first

Nobody hands the lead to the new person because of a job title. It came from doing the daily work well: asking good questions early, backing decisions with research, and presenting the work clearly. GM's team came to trust that, and the role grew.

That trust carried further. GM's own UX team later appointed me lead designer on a second transformation project, with two of its members supporting me. I stepped in halfway and took it through to its first handoff with IT.

## Rebuilding the store around a new catalog

A catalog change can look like a content update. In practice it's a redesign, because plans, add-ons, and prices decide how a customer understands what they're buying.

The work ran from individual plan pages through the cart and checkout. I also worked on how subscription pricing should be displayed, and plan details went through user testing along the way.

## What changed

We launched more than 18 Owner Center shopping pages built on the new catalog. Weekly site views rose by more than 30,000 at their peak and held at least 20,000 above where they had been.

## What I took from it

A new title gives you permission. It doesn't give you trust. On an established team, the fastest way to earn the lead is to be the person whose work nobody has to double-check.
