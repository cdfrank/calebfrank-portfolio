A test drive usually asks you to go to the dealership, on its schedule, and deal with whoever is on the floor. Test Drive My Way flipped that. A Chevrolet customer could book a test drive and have the car come to them.

Chevrolet took the idea live with the Bolt EV in San Francisco, Los Angeles, and San Diego in March 2017. For me, this project was the turn from print to product. It had the same dealer audience I already knew, in a new medium.

## Two customers, one experience

A service like this has two sides. Customers needed an easy way to book. Dealers needed to know what was expected of them and how to deliver it.

I designed across both, from the customer and dealer experience to the pilot materials and a dashboard for tracking how it ran. The clients liked the dashboard. That reaction mattered less than what it let us see.

## What the pilot taught us

A real pilot corrects your assumptions quickly. Three findings stood out.

**Saturday was the peak.** People's free time set the demand, so the service had to be ready for it.

**An hour with a customer took four hours of dispatch.** A delightful experience for one person carried real operational cost for the dealer. That's easy to miss when you only look at the front end.

**People wanted to book first.** Mobile users tried to book before giving personal information. The order mattered to them: first show me it will work, then ask who I am.

## In their words

Customer feedback was warm, and revealing:

> "Loved it! I wish that this was a concierge service available for purchasing all new cars!"

Another customer wanted something between the app and the store:

> "It would be neat if there was also person assigned to call to with follow-up questions. I really don't want to call the dealer yet."

That second quote stayed with me. People wanted a person, just not the pressure that came with one. In our research they put it plainly: respect my time, and let me stay in control.

## What changed

The findings fed the executive readout and later scheduling work at Lab313, where a test-drive scheduler kicked off in 2018.

## What I took from it

A pilot is the most honest prototype there is. It also gave me a question I still work on at STELLA: how can a dealership be available on the customer's terms, without the customer doing all the work?
