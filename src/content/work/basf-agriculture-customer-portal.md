BASF sold agricultural products through a chain of partners, so it rarely dealt directly with the people at the end of that chain. By 2021, SalesX had given its own sales teams a unified platform. The next question was what partners and customers should get.

The answer became a suite of portals. For the Customer Portal, it was also new ground: BASF's first time doing business directly with customers.

## What makes a portal worth opening?

A portal is easy to request and easy to ignore. So before designing screens, we defined what would earn repeat visits. We came down to two jobs: save people time, and deepen the relationship.

Saving time meant bringing tools together, surfacing high-value content, reducing sign-in friction, and giving people digital access to their history. Deepening the relationship meant useful recommendations, exclusive content or tools, and a connection to people who could help.

A January 2021 heuristic and competitive review turned that into five layers, each building on the last: a solid foundation, connections that matter, meaningful interactions, personalized experiences, and ongoing measurement.

## The Partner Portal: from basics to transparency

Our goal was transparency between BASF and its business partners. At launch, partners could only see basic account information. Over the next phases, they gained financials, orders, business activity, product education, and specialized tools.

Two additions stood out. We connected a learning management system, so training lived inside the portal. We also showed sales data in several views, split by volume and sales and by month, season, calendar, or year over year. A grower's business runs on seasons, so a calendar view alone wouldn't have told the whole story.

## The Customer Portal: starting a new relationship

For customers, the portal brought together education on government regulations, business activity, sales figures, purchases, and orders. Regulations mattered here. Agricultural products come with real compliance questions, and clear guidance gives people a reason to return.

None of this worked without one identity. I had already designed a unified login, which we extended across the US Crop, P&SS, and Seed business segments, so one business relationship didn't mean three separate sign-ins.

## What changed

The Partner Portal launched and kept growing. The first stage of the Customer Portal was designed and built. The work also opened the door for MRM's strategy and CX teams to join the planning, and the BASF account grew beyond tools.

## What I took from it

Asking "what would make this worth opening?" is a better start than "what should it contain?" It also helped to know the subject. Time in agriculture outside of work meant I understood why a season can matter more than a quarter.
