In 2018, buying a car online still ended the same way: at a dealership, starting over. MyCarDrive was a GM Lab313 concept for changing that. Shoppers could price a vehicle, message a dealer, and share their documents online, then finish online or in the store.

We could have tested whether people liked it. We tested whether we were right.

## We wrote down what we believed

Before talking to anyone, the team wrote 13 hypotheses across the four parts of the prototype. Writing them down mattered, because it made it harder to hear only what we hoped to hear. On October 1 and 2, 2018, we took the prototype into one-on-one interviews with more than 10 customers.

Seven held up. Six didn't.

## The misses taught us the most

The concept tested well. People felt it would save time and liked seeing how a vehicle's price became a monthly payment. The challenged hypotheses were more useful, because most of them came down to trust.

**People didn't know who was on the other end.** In the messenger, some believed they were talking to their salesperson. Others assumed a call center. Nothing on the screen settled it, and they were willing to share less as the subject grew more sensitive.

**People didn't believe the price would survive the trip.** Newer shoppers expected the dealer to honor what they saw online. Experienced shoppers assumed they'd start over at the dealership, or that better deals were being held back.

**People wanted control, not just convenience.** Most were fine sharing with the dealership they were working with, but only with the people on their deal. Some wanted documents deleted after the sale. Others wanted to keep them for next time. Many weren't sure why a document was needed at all.

> Trust wasn't a feature we could add at the end. It was the product.

## Then we asked the dealers

Customers were only half of the conversation, so we took the concept to dealer-facing teams. Their questions were just as direct. Who answers a chat, sales or the BDC? Who sets an appointment? Should one dealer see what a customer did with another? How do we keep dealers from having too much access?

Those sessions turned a messaging feature into a service design problem. A message is only as good as the person and process behind it.

## From findings to a plan

Each result came with a next step. We separated pricing tools from vehicle-specific ones, because customers wanted to budget before choosing a car. We planned clearer labels for fees, new scenarios for the messenger, and privacy settings for documents.

We also wrote usage narratives to test the experience as a whole. An independent analyst review backed the direction, and it pushed us to set expectations earlier and let people see how their inputs changed what came next. That shaped the Dealer Connect strategy for messaging, scheduling, and documents.

## The same questions, eight years later

I'm now a Product Manager at STELLA Automotive AI, working on voice AI for car dealerships. The technology is new, but customers are asking what they asked in 2018.

## What I took from it

Write down what you believe before you test it. The hypotheses you lose will teach you more than the ones you win, and sometimes they stay useful for the rest of your career.
